DL977, Boston - San Francisco, May 5th, 2026

Everytime when I start to write, I'll check when and what I wrote last time. The last time of this time was more than two years ago (sry no English version last time). I didn't explicitly mention, but I had a disastrous winter break then: Got a warning letter from the department, was thinking about whether I should continue my study, lost my passport in Europe so had to go back to China, and lost members from my family and school.

I thought a bit more when I was waiting for my passport and visa at home. Figured that my good friends were gonna graduate in that coming semester, and I probably wouldn't meet them again if I didn't go back to school. So I bought the ticket back to US in the end. I stared at the campus through the window when I finally got back to my room, thinking that I had to manage through several more semesters than my friends, and that I'd probably jump out of this window one day.

I'm not a mentally stable person. Sometimes I think about how I can die. For example I think about if I want to jump down from a building, I need to overcome the physical rejection from mental fear to step out. Then the sense of zero gravity comes. I feel the sense itself doesn't bring fear in this phase for me. I have experienced it when I reckless skied over a big park jump. I realized that the ground seemed a bit far from myself, and it was coming to me rapidly. It was fear from not knowing how to deal with it and realizing it wouldn't end well. The last thing will be touching ground, where the brain should receive an instant hit. The speed of breaking the brain might be faster than the speed of neurotransmission. At this point I would be distracted and thinking about the structure of the brain instead of the original sadness.

Obviously I didn't jump out of that window in the end. I graduated and moved out. As a person who had lived in a 4 m2 (43 sqft) space, I kinda like that room.

The title "What the World Gifted Me" is a song that 王菲 (Faye Wong, a famous Chinese singer) sang on the Lunar New Year show a year later. After that sad winter break, I was honored to receive many gifts and opportunities. Yiwei grabbed me to corners of Europe, Wangli introduced new good friends to me, Mateo brought me opportunities to show my skills... I won an award, finished the long degree, and this also include being invited to the event this time in San Francisco.

I feel I can say I've made approriate efforts to value these favors. Were there better choices for some decisions? Maybe. Were there works I could do better? Maybe. But I feel I've gained a better mental model for working, and won't think much about past things too much. I know I can learn specific domains of knowledge faster, and work on specific areas faster, like my intern seemed to have a lot of works to do, but I was only fully working for half of the in-office time. However, if we talk about being actively hardworking in school, I might be able to finish an 8hr job in 4hr, but I can't work for 8hr straight either. I'll procrastinate and get distracted. So I'm not getting an overall faster progression than most of the people. I know people much smarter than me, much more hardworking than me, or much more successful than me, and I envy about the fact that I might be as outstanding as them if I work more. But I slowly started to tell myself that I've tried my best and what I'm getting is what I deserve.

Nevertheless, I still remember the night when I received the offer from MIT. Though I didn't get the opportunity to stay as a phd, thanks the world for giving me these four years of dream.

DL495, Seattle - Boston, May 25th, 2026

Thanks Yinign and Biru for referring me jobs, and freed me from the anxiety of no result in phd application and job hunting.

One of the job is the closest to my childhood dream, building cars. I had the dream all the way to college. I always used to check all the aspects of new cars in the market, appearance, functions, handlings (of course through video games at that time). I thought about studying car design or engineering, and obviously there was no way I can be a race driver considering my family's financial situation. But the result of college entrance exam happened to throw me to a place where there were no car-related majors. The final decision of choosing architecture was solely because I learned from a documentary that there was a Maserati designer who had architecture background. I considered for 30 seconds: it was really hard for me to force myself into a major about cars, and architecture looked fun and allowed switching to a lot of other areas. So I devoted myself in architecture. Seeing from now I made a correct bet: this car company in Shanghai hoped me as an architecture student to design interior interaction for them.

But in the end I accepted the offer from another company in Bay Area. I felt the decision is quite similar to when I decided to study architecture: I can decide to switch country anytime if I stay in US, but not if I go back to China, and I can earn more and get myself a better car first. The job is to build AI software to aid architectural design. On one hand, I indeed met the promise in my personal statement for applying MArch "wishing to bring benefits to the field of architecture with computer technologies". On the other hand, I feel sad after chatting with classmates: some friends perform much better in class than me, worked harder than me, but could only get a junior designer job with less pay than me, and get worried if companies like the one I'm joining would replace them with AI. I don't know what I can do about it, and don't want to discuss topics about capitalism. I just hope everyone gets a better life in the future, and the world can be more fair.

UA858, Shanghai - San Francisco, June 19th, 2026

Video games are fascinating because in the virtual world, put aside some plot settings, effort will always pay off. As simple as Stardew Valley and Minecraft: Players can always gain something and improve the virtual life if they farm, mine, and kill monsters everyday. Even for probability-based games like Balatro, you will surely get all achievements one day if you believe in the probability model and design reliable strategies. But the real life is obviously much more complicated, and the most imbalanced aspect is the starting point. The difference between people in childhood might be as simple as brain powers: I can understand a concept about physics in 5 minutes, but I can't memorize all the required poems even till the college entrance exam. And another person might be able to understand what a poem expresses after 5 minutes of reading, but can't figure out how to use some formulas till the exam. And it's even more decisive after becoming adults when we ought to be independent: I can casually agree to move to Hong Kong for college, but some people might not be able to afford a tour to Hong Kong. On the other hand, some other people might just move to US from childhood, buy my dream car as soon as they get the driver licenses, and adapt to the diversified society. Years ago I hate those people who gain recognitions and achievements from selling themselves like a lot of my hardworking friends. But after I thought twice, I feel they are not bad, as they've made their effort, and it's just their effort doesn't quite fit our value. From another aspect, ideologies like communism might be able to reset the society into a structure where everyone has the same social status, but they can't turn people into teh same kind of human. People have different brains, so they would surely be good at different things, and different expectations to the world, including the hope for a free society without communism. The world can be a game, but it can't be a machine.

BA286 + BA608 + BA740 + LX1666 (?), San Francisco - Venice, Sept 4th, 2026

I didn't think I could postpone publishing this page for so long. I always felt I missed something I could talk about. Un the blink of an eye I've lived in Bay Area for two months, and am always getting new conversations and thoughts.

Celebrated my 26th birthday, but I still don't have particular feeling about the number now. I've started to think about death again recently, but not like that kind in the beginning. It's rather about the sense of time flowing faster when one gets older. I might die from aging when I'm 80, or earlier, since we should have died around 50 but human lives are extended by modern technologies. Either 50 or 80, it could be tomorrow, a glimpse, in an accelerated sense of time. I don't reject dying tomorrow, but how much I should do to avoid dying with regret or frustration? On one hand, I'm satisfied with my strength and past achievements as I mentioned earlier. On the other hand, I still have many decisions and much effort to make.

This seems to bring me some sort of unnecessary anxiety. I know a lot of friends who worked for years, and they just relax after office and in the weekends, like watching dramas, hanging around, or draw some paintings. Bt I always feel I have some more important things I should do. I used "unnecessary" since I'm often not actually doing something important, because I didn't plan any. And anxiety, as a negative mood, would only bring more procrastination and more phone scrolling.

I'm happy that I was born in 2000, not earlier or later. I could slowly digest the development of internet and machine intelligence in my childhood like studying everything else, as well as learn about how the slower technologies ran the world in 20th century out of curiosity. "New technologies during youth are sci-fi, new technologies during 20s and 30s are the destined direction of progression, new technologies in later life are over-aggressive and should be banned". I hope my judgement won't move to the third phase, but that requires one to respond correctly to new technologies keeping emerging faster and faster, which is a difficult thing.

GPT-6 was released yesterday, and I saw another wave of posts demonstrating applications in some fields. I would say that barriers for all technical stuffs would only be lower and lower, and I was lucky to switch to design as something I love. When all the technological problems can be solved instantly, the only aspect that requires human is how to serve human better. AI can optimize the society, but as I mentioned society shouldn't be a machine. The world needs the feeling of humanity, like art, entertainment, aesthetic, social connection, and one core of them, novelty. LLM can solve a lot of things technically, like implementing software, generating videos. But the novelty assembled from dataset would always be small and temporary. The true novelty comes from creation, an act can't be expressed by literal language. It will always be competitions in a capitalist society, and when technological strengths become instantly the same for everyone, the competition would be about whose creation people appreciate more.
